# Raw notes: saving cards for returning customers

**Fictional example.** All names, quotes and numbers here are made up, to try the research stage.

## Interview 1 · Michelle S., 34, orders every two weeks
- Orders mostly from her phone on the bus to work.
- "Every time I have to get up and find my wallet, so I just leave it in the cart and order in the evening from the computer"
- Asked whether it could "remember me like Amazon does".
- When we suggested saving a card: "Only if I can see where it's saved and delete it".
- Phone for follow-up: 052-7781234

## Interview 2 · Joe B., 51, orders once a month
- Doesn't feel that typing the card is a problem. "It takes a minute, what's the big deal".
- Wouldn't save a card on the site "of a small shop". His card was hacked two years ago.
- Uses Apple Pay in other apps: "if there was a button like that I'd press it".

## Interview 3 · Nora L., 28
- Orders from her phone, usually in the evening on the couch.
- Typed the card number wrong twice, and the system "threw me back to the start".
- "That's the moment I say: fine, later". Didn't come back to the order.
- Would save a card: "sure, like everywhere".

## Interview 4 · David K., 45, orders for the whole family
- Has two cards, personal and business, and wants to choose each time.
- "If it only saves one and I have to switch every time, that's worse".
- Couldn't remember if he ever paid with Apple Pay.

## Interview 5 · Sarah M., 39
- Orders from her work computer, almost never from her phone.
- Not relevant to the mobile problem. Mostly asked for faster delivery.

## Support tickets, last quarter (support team summary)
- 200 tickets about payment.
- 46 of them: "the payment didn't go through" after card details were typed wrong.
- 9 of them ask whether a card can be saved.
- 3 ask about Apple Pay.

## Data (data team, 3 months)
- 61% of returning customers' orders start on a phone.
- Of returning customers who reach payment on a phone, 38% don't complete the order in the same session.
- Unknown how many of them complete it later on a computer.

## Interviewer's notes
- In interviews 1 and 3 there was clear frustration at the moment of typing the card.
- Only interview 2 raised a security concern explicitly, but Michelle (interview 1) also made it conditional on transparency.
